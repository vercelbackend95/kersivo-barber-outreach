## In short

A marketplace (Booksy, Fresha, Treatwell, Vagaro) is a shop window: people who have never heard of you can find you there, and most marketplaces charge a fee when they do. Your own booking website is the front door for clients who already know you. The best set-up for most independent shops is to let the marketplace bring first visits, then make every repeat booking happen through a link you own.

## What a marketplace is good at

- **Being found by strangers.** People browse a marketplace app by area and service. If your shop is new, or you are in a busy high street, that traffic is real.
- **Reviews in one place.** Clients leave reviews where they booked.
- **Low effort to start.** You get a listing page without building anything.

## What it costs when a marketplace finds a client

Each marketplace sets its own rules for "new client" fees. On their published UK terms:

- **Booksy:** standard marketplace bookings carry no extra fee. Boost, the optional promotion, charges 30% of the first visit (minimum £5) for a new client it brings. *(Checked 15 November 2026.)*
- **Fresha:** a new client found through the Fresha marketplace costs 20% of the first booking (minimum £4). Bookings through your own Fresha booking link carry no marketplace fee. *(Checked 15 November 2026.)*
- **Treatwell:** commission on a new marketplace client is 35%, subject to location and your partner agreement. Direct bookings through your own Treatwell link carry no marketplace commission; online prepayment processing may apply. *(Checked 8 October 2026.)*
- **Vagaro:** in the UK, a qualifying new client's first booking through the marketplace costs 20% of the booking amount, excluding VAT. *(Checked 8 October 2026.)*

On a £25 cut, that first visit can cost from nothing to £8.75 in fees, before card processing. The full table with each source is on the [new-client fees comparison](/compare#new-client-fees).

## What your own booking website is good at

- **Repeat clients book you, not a list of shops.** On a marketplace app, your regular sees other barbers nearby every time they rebook. On your own page they see only you.
- **Your name in Google.** A website on your own domain builds your shop's name in search, separate from any platform.
- **You keep the client list.** Check who can export the client list, and in what format, before you commit to any system.
- **One link everywhere.** The same booking link goes in your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile and on the till.

## How to use both without paying twice

1. Treat the marketplace as advertising. If a platform charges for new clients, count that fee as the cost of acquiring a client, the same way you would count a flyer or an ad.
2. After the first visit, give every client your own booking link: on the receipt, in the confirmation message, on a card at the mirror.
3. Put your own link first on Instagram and on your Google Business Profile. Keep the marketplace listing for people who find you there.
4. Check each platform's rules on direct links. Fresha and Treatwell, for example, do not charge marketplace fees on bookings through your own link on their system. Read how each platform decides who counts as a "new" client.
5. Every few months, look at where repeat bookings come from. If most still come through the marketplace app, your own link is not visible enough.

## When a marketplace alone is enough

If you are starting out, have no website and need first clients, a marketplace listing is a reasonable start. The question is what happens on the second visit.

## When your own booking website matters most

- You already have a steady base of regulars.
- Most of your new clients come from walk-ins, word of mouth, Instagram or Google rather than the marketplace app.
- You want your shop's name, not a platform's, to be what people search for.
